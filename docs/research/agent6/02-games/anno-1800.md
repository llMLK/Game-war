## A — System model
**EXTERNAL-EVIDENCE AN-C1:** island fertility and supply chains connect production, population needs and shipping. Player specializes islands, constructs capacity and routes goods; availability, demand and operating costs constrain expansion. Stocks/income feed back into continued growth; missing goods can cascade across dependent chains. Continuous economy with pause/management rather than campaign turns. Exact ratios/shipping rules are outside this bounded dossier. [AN3](https://www.pcgamer.com/anno-1800-review/)

## B — Why it works
**ANALYST-INFERENCE — operational outcome/scale:** a supply network succeeds when delivered goods support the chosen population/expansion goal at sustainable cost; shortages and destabilized dependent production are failure states for that network. This is not an invented universal sandbox loss rule. Shipping and demand evolve continuously; a single balance snapshot is weaker than trend/stock evidence.

**ANALYST-INFERENCE:** location matters when one city cannot efficiently supply everything. A secure route can be more valuable than one more production building. Mature growth can add new dependencies and investment choices rather than only repeat early construction.

## C/D — Failures and counterexamples
**EXTERNAL-EVIDENCE AN-C3:** Rick Lane found fluctuating finances hard to diagnose and wished for graphs, while explicitly saying he did not feel bogged down in micro; he also criticized repeated AI chatter. **AN-C5:** preGU6 players ask how to locate production/route problems. Those are independent perspectives with different experience. [AN5](https://www.reddit.com/r/anno/comments/cy8lf2)
**ANALYST-INFERENCE FM-ECON-OVERLOAD/FM-REPETITION:** delays between demand, dispatch and delivery can make a treasury snapshot misleading; notifications distract if repeated without a new decision. More detail is enjoyable to some players and a burden to others.
