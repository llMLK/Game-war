# Leader research and interpretation notes

These notes are for development. References, citation language and source links are deliberately absent from player-facing profiles, candidate cards and dialogue.

## Narrative baseline

The western campaign presents the political setting of 715 CE, and the eastern campaign begins in 208 CE. An initial biography describes experience and position known at that point. A later candidate describes his background by his first availability year. It does not forecast later battles, accessions, betrayals or death. Actual campaign records are shown separately from the short biography.

- Maslama is already an experienced frontier commander by 715; later famous campaigns do not become biography spoilers. His initial fame is renowned rather than a retrospective maximum.
- Musa and Tariq already have western-campaign reputations. Their availability is short and linked to western territories or the Damascus court. Abd al-Aziz ibn Musa begins with the existing Seville army rather than in a recruitable pool while someone else commands his post.
- Abd al-Malik ibn Rifa'a belongs to the Egyptian jund and is appropriate to the Fustat post in 715. His two-sentence biography stops at his present appointment and prior service.
- The Sicily commander named in the atlas is not independently established here for 715. The new-game leader binding therefore uses a modest local garrison officer instead of fabricating a biography or moving the emperor there.
- The Khazar ruler's personal identity is left unspecified. The office is represented as `خاقان الخزر`; a disputed later identity is not imposed on 715.
- Zhao Yun, Guan Yu, Zhou Yu, Zhuge Liang and other eastern leaders are distinguished by experience, current position and relationships rather than future victories. Early candidates such as Sima Yi do not arrive with their later careers already earned.
- Ma Chao's Shu invitation begins in 214; regional recruitment also requires access to the relevant western posts. Jiang Wei and Wang Ping likewise require the campaign to reach their border region. Broad invitation windows allow alternate campaign outcomes, but do not claim a historically inevitable change of service.

- Hugh Kennedy, *Egypt as a province in the Islamic caliphate, 641–868*, in *The Cambridge History of Egypt*, vol. 1 (1998), p. 73, as identified by bibliographic cross-references for Egyptian jund appointments; no claim of direct full-book inspection.

## Simulation boundaries

Aptitudes, loyalty, prestige thresholds, salaries, personality modifiers and portrait geometry are authored game design. They are not measured historical facts. Relationship seeds use well-established companionship or rivalry; new campaign events can change them. Regional ownership is a gameplay condition for contact, not a claim that an individual must historically have served whoever owns that city.

Illustrations are coherent procedural busts, not authentic portraits. Minor local officers have modest prestige and local-service biographies. Legacy fictional names remain identifiable in old saves but do not enter new historical candidate pools. Historical death dates bound new recruitment eligibility; an employed leader's later life is governed by the alternate campaign rather than a disclosed historical obituary.
