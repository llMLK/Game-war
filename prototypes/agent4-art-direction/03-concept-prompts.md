# Constantinople concept prompts A–E

Version 1.0 • 715 CE • Imperial Illuminated Atlas

**Written prompts only. Do not execute until the owner explicitly approves the direction and authorizes first concept production.** English prompt bodies are supplied for direct generator use. Arabic captions, labels and UI are added separately, never asked of the image model. Prompt output is a concept requiring paintover, not a guaranteed reusable asset.

## Shared reference protocol

At first generation there is no approved image. Supply an artist-built camera/topology guide and dated architectural references with known usage rights. When A is approved, save its palette/brushwork crop as STYLE-01; when B's geometry is approved, save its blockout as CONST-01. Use the same references in C–E. These identifiers describe **future deliverables**, not files that currently exist.

The camera is an orthographic, isometric-inspired 2.5D view, elevation 35°, from southwest toward northeast. North projects upper-left; east upper-right. Light enters upper-left, shadows run lower-right. Preserve broad geography: land walls west/lower-left, Golden Horn north/upper-left, Marmara south/lower-right, eastern peninsula tip upper-right. Historical geography overrides an attractive alternative arrangement.

Each prompt below is self-contained. References strengthen it but never replace the explicit constraints. Set the generator's actual transparency option for B/D/E if supported; merely writing “transparent” is insufficient. For a tool lacking alpha, produce a plain neutral background for supervised extraction, mark alpha pending, and do not call that output production-ready.

## A — Signature artistic masterpiece

**Use:** establish the first style anchor for owner review. One landscape illustration, proposed 3:2 composition, high-resolution authoring target 3072×2048; use the generator's supported size and record it honestly.

**Prompt:**

Create a signature painted atlas plate of Constantinople in 715 CE for Suyuf Al-Mamalik. The intended use is a master art-direction concept, not a map screenshot. Fuse physically convincing historical architecture with the controlled pigment, hand-shaped edges and sparse metallic illumination of a precious manuscript. Make that synthesis visible within the city, not just in a frame.

Use a parallel-projection elevated view at 35 degrees, looking northeast from southwest; no wide-angle lens and no converging verticals. North is upper-left and east upper-right. Arrange the western land defenses across the lower-left approach; the Golden Horn along the upper-left/northern edge; Marmara along the lower-right/southern edge. The peninsula narrows toward upper-right. Keep Hagia Sophia in the eastern monumental district, the Hippodrome southwest of it and a lower imperial precinct southeast of it. Compress distances gently while preserving adjacency and water/land relationships. This is a historically informed schematic composition, not an exact aerial survey.

Lead the eye through a deep masonry gateway, across irregular red-roofed neighborhoods and open courts, toward Hagia Sophia's broad dome, semi-domes and substantial supporting volumes. Counterbalance its summit with the long defensive rhythm and a quiet dark-blue water shape. Include a few modest timber vessels and working quay details; small inhabitants establish scale only in the middle and foreground. Leave cultivated/open patches among peripheral districts; do not fill every inch with identical houses. Differentiate land defenses from the lower sea-wall edge. Avoid a generic western castle as the palace.

Paint in a digital tempera-and-gouache manner with thin glazed shadows, opaque luminous stone planes, selective fine dry-brush masonry marks and restrained gold-leaf-like accents. Describe limestone joints only where they turn a corner or frame an opening. Give terracotta roofs distinct ridges and eaves, plaster subtle localized wear, timber visible structural purpose, linen a few taut folds. The buildings must have weight and inhabited use, without plastic bevels or a universal ruin patina.

Light comes from screen upper-left, warm broad daylight with cool sky fill; all cast shadows run lower-right. Preserve the gate opening and dome edge as focal dark/light transitions. Use aged ivory #E9DFC8/#C7BDA7, terracotta #A95E45, deep lapis water #36586C, muted turquoise #688F89 near selected shallows, olive #727C59, restrained crimson #883F40 and purple #65506D. Gold #B99449 with small #EEDB98 highlights occupies no more than 3 percent of the painting and is clearly an illustrative finish, never a wholly gold dome or gold roofscape.

Heighten grandeur through controlled value grouping, breadth of masonry, rich material transitions and a generous breathing space around the chief landmark. Keep landmark linear enlargement within about 15 percent of the supplied schematic guide; do not invent taller spires. Retain approximately two-thirds calm masses and concentrate small marks near the focal route. Use a fully painted landscape background with quiet parchment-toned margins; no transparency. Any narrow ornamental edge must remain subordinate and use abstract, period-informed motifs without pseudo-inscriptions. Do not place words or labels inside the artwork.

Keep the camera, light direction, palette and landmark arrangement suitable to become a reference for compact and expanded game art. Exclude Ottoman minarets, later Ottoman palaces, Gothic towers, Renaissance architecture, artillery bastions, modern waterfronts, late sailing ships, fantasy or magical glow, game logos, copied commercial artwork, toy miniatures, low-poly or voxel rendering, plastic gloss, oversaturated mobile-game colors, decorative clutter and fake Arabic or Greek writing. Do not depict the later siege of 717–718 as the current event.

**Review focus:** the city's authored composition and unique identity must earn approval before technical asset work. A successful grand image does not automatically approve B.
